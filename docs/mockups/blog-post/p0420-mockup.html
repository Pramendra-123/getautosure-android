P0420 Code: What It Means, Repair Cost in India, and Is It Safe to Drive?

Quick answer

P0420 means the catalytic converter isn't cleaning the exhaust as well as it should. It's usually safe to drive for a few weeks, but get it checked: often the cause is a ₹2,500–₹8,000 oxygen sensor or an exhaust leak, not the ₹25,000+ converter itself.

What does P0420 mean?

Your car has two oxygen sensors: one before the catalytic converter and one after it. The engine computer compares them. When the rear sensor starts looking almost the same as the front one, the converter isn't doing its job, and the computer stores P0420 – Catalyst efficiency below threshold (Bank 1) and turns on the check-engine light.

What causes it?

In rough order of how often we see it on Indian roads:

  1. Faulty rear oxygen sensor: it misreads, and the converter is actually fine.
  2. Exhaust leak near the sensors, common after a rough speed-breaker hit.
  3. Engine misfires or rich running that overheat and damage the converter (fix these first, or a new converter will fail too).
  4. Worn-out catalytic converter: usual after 1–1.5 lakh km, sooner on CNG-converted cars.

Is it safe to drive with P0420?

Usually yes, for a short while. The car won't break down from P0420 alone. But don't ignore it: you'll likely fail the PUC test, and if misfires are the cause, they'll keep damaging the new converter. If the check-engine light is flashing, that's a misfire: stop and get it checked now.

Frequently asked questions

Will clearing the P0420 code fix it?

No. It hides the light until the computer re-tests, usually within a few drives. The cause is still there.

Does P0420 fail the PUC test?

Often yes, because a weak converter raises CO and HC readings.

Is P0420 common on CNG cars?

Yes. CNG burns hotter, which can age the converter faster, especially on retro-fitted kits.

Can I just remove the catalytic converter?

No. It's illegal for road use in India, fails PUC, and the check-engine light will stay on.
