The odometer becomes a required step

At the moment, km is an optional box under the RTO record, so it's easy to miss. If it's left blank, the price assumes an average car: a car with 1 lakh km gets priced like one with 30,000. In the new step, the buyer can't reach the look-around or the price until the km is in. They type the exact reading because we need it to catch a rolled-back odometer. If the dashboard is dead or blank, they pick a range instead.

What changes behind the screen

  • "Next" is locked until there's a km reading or a range. Demo mode fills one in.
  • The AI market price and our formula both wait for the km. No price is shown without it.
  • A range is priced at its middle (30–50k becomes 40,000 km). The report says "30–50k km (seller's word, not checked)".
  • The km-per-year line uses the RTO registration date. Over 20,000 a year is called out. Under 3,000 a year on a car older than three years is flagged "unusually low, check the service book", a common sign of a rolled-back odometer.
  • A dashboard that doesn't match the car's computer stays a named red flag, as it is today.
